# Limitations

This document states what the results in this repository do and do not establish. Read it before using the numbers.

## Scope of the claim

The paper asserts that a horizonless ultracompact branch exists in the solution family generated by the curvature tower, that its mass range overlaps the lower black-hole mass gap, and that its gravitational-wave signatures are computable and currently consistent with published limits. It does not assert that any observed object is a branch member. The branch is a possibility consistent with current limits, not a detection.

The scope narrowed during the work. The two-horizon branch of the same family is classically transient, with a mass-inflation e-fold time of 0.02 to 0.04 ms, and curvature capping and a non-degenerate outer structure are not jointly satisfiable in any two-horizon regime examined. The object of the paper is the mass-gap and horizonless branch, not a model of astrophysical black holes.

The tower is imported rather than derived, and is strictly defined only in dimension five and above; its four-dimensional use is a phenomenological resummation. The cap psi < 1/alpha, the identity with the Hayward control metric and the mini-theorem below survive that restriction, since they depend only on the signs of the couplings. No claim that the metric solves a specific four-dimensional action does.

## The mini-theorem is a no-go, not a construction

The result recorded as E4 states that no tower with alpha_1 = 1 and all further couplings non-negative admits a degenerate inner horizon. On the root of the first condition the second reduces to E2 = (4 psi*^2 / 9h)(2 psi* h'' - h'), bounded below by 4 psi*^2 / 9h and therefore strictly positive. The identities were checked symbolically with zero residuals, and evaluation over geometric, n alpha^(n-1), logarithmic, two-term and fifteen mixed towers returned E2 between +0.089 and +0.458 with no candidate found. This is a negative result about a class; it says nothing about stability or dynamics, and it does not forbid degenerate inner horizons in general. A published construction achieves one at the price of a strong transverse null-energy violation inside the core.

## The threshold is a range, not a number

The branch criterion is M sqrt(eps_c) < c_ext, and its inversion M_crit = c_ext / sqrt(eps_c) gives the upper mass of the horizonless branch. The shape factor c_ext depends on the sharpness of the density profile and runs over [0.18, 0.39]; the reference profile with n = 6 gives 0.2314. The threshold mass therefore carries a factor of about 2.2 of profile uncertainty at fixed transition density, and quoting a single threshold mass is incorrect.

| eps_c (multiples of nuclear saturation, 2.8e14 g/cm^3) | M_crit, solar masses |
|---|---|
| 1 | 8.45 to 18.3 |
| 4.7 | 3.90 (n = 6: 5.01) to 8.45 |
| 10 | 2.7 to 5.8 |
| 22 | 1.8 to 3.9 |

At eps_c = 5 times nuclear the same calculation gives 4.95 for n = 6, 6.8 for n near 4, and 8.43 for n = 2. The spread is a property of the family rather than a numerical error bar, and it is large enough to open and close a physical window. Radial stability at ten solar masses requires eps_c of at least 29.25 times nuclear, while horizonlessness of a 2.6 solar-mass object requires at most 17.5 times. At the nominal shape factor the joint window is empty; it opens only for c_ext above about 0.32.

## Echo amplitudes were corrected

The echo amplitude ratios first reported for M/M_ext = 0.88, 0.95 and 0.995, namely 0.081, 0.283 and 0.321, are wrong. Windows placed at integer multiples of the geometric-optics delay captured the tail of the previous packet under packet drift, producing degenerate repeating ratios and trains that did not decay. Those values are superseded.

With windows centred on power maxima the trains decay physically. The corrected first-echo ratios at unit wall reflectivity are 0.59 to 0.69 at 0.995 and 0.32 to 0.56 at 0.95; the band reflects a factor 1.7 disagreement between the energy and envelope estimators at 0.95, which vanishes at 0.995. The 0.88 configuration has a shallow cavity, mixed packet intervals and a comb spacing off by 14 percent, so its amplitude is unreliable and no value is quoted.

The correction reverses an observational statement. At 0.995 the corrected amplitude exceeds the published limit of 0.42 whenever reflectivity is above about 0.6, so that configuration is excluded by existing data, and 0.95 is marginal. Periods, comb spacings and delays of 2.0 to 20 ms across 10 to 60 solar masses are unaffected. Reflectivity is itself unconstrained, so the exclusion is conditional on it.

## The f-mode computation depends on third-party machinery

The f-modes were computed with RelModPy (Fabian Gittins, MIT licence), vendored under `src/third_party/relmodpy`. Its Muller root finder diverges from imprecise starting points, running away to three distinct spurious roots in the diagnostics, and was replaced by a scan of |A_in| over the real frequency followed by a two-dimensional Nelder-Mead minimisation. The equation-of-state adapters are ours. Two anchors support the replacement: the library author's published anchor is reproduced at Re(omega M) = 0.17084 against 0.171, a discrepancy of 0.09 percent, and the SLy f-mode at 1.4 solar masses comes out at 1.899 kHz, inside the literature band of 1.8 to 2.1 kHz.

The corner-branch sequence is nevertheless non-monotonic: 2.161 kHz at 1.8 solar masses, 2.298 kHz at 2.2, and 1.926 kHz at 3.0. All three roots are stable, but the non-monotonicity is flagged rather than explained, with interface modes of the analogue core the suspected contaminant. The root at 2.6 solar masses initially failed to localise because the scan window was capped at omega M = 0.16 while the root sits near 0.178. With the window widened to 0.10 to 0.24 the run converged at |A_in| near 1e-11, giving 2.222, 2.204 and 2.184 kHz at 2.59, 2.63 and 2.67 solar masses, insensitive to the core adiabatic index over three orders of magnitude.

## The universal relation does not discriminate the branch

The universal relation between f-mode frequency, tidal deformability and mass is not violated. At 1.8 solar masses the branch gives 2.161 kHz against the relation's 2.170 kHz, an agreement of 0.4 percent, with deformabilities of 48.7 and 51. F-mode asteroseismology therefore cannot separate this branch from an ordinary compact star. The discriminating observables are the mass range and the shape of the deformability curve, which falls from 93.5 at 1.68 solar masses to near zero at 3.47.

## What the branch does not explain

The branch populates roughly 2.1 to 3.5 solar masses. It does not explain why the region from 3.5 to 5 solar masses is observed to be nearly empty, and it makes no prediction about how populated the branch is. The formation-channel estimate, a few percent of core collapses plus neutron-star mergers at a rate lower by two to three orders of magnitude, is an order-of-magnitude census marked as hypothesis; no population synthesis was performed, and merger remnant dynamics lie outside the calculation.

## Quasinormal modes and the halo criterion

The direct quasinormal-mode shifts of the two-horizon branch of the n = 6 family are zero to better than 1e-6 across the full range of the length parameter examined. The reason is structural: the exponentially compact profile holds essentially all the mass inside the outer root, so the potential barrier sits where the metric function has already relaxed to Schwarzschild. The 0.087 (l/M)^2 law is a law of halo profiles, not of this family; a reference profile carrying 0.77 percent of its mass outside the outer root gives a shift of -0.17 percent.

An earlier table quoting static corrections of 1.1 to 4.6 percent, and a related figure of 2.29 percent falling to 0.06 percent, applied that halo law to a family it does not describe, and is superseded. The ringdown places no lower bound on the transition density. Distinguishing zero from -0.17 percent requires a fractional accuracy near 0.1 percent; single current detections deliver tens of percent and next-generation instruments around 0.5 to 2 percent on loud events, so the test requires stacking on the order of twenty-five to four hundred events.

## The GW170817 constraints and their assumptions

The baryon budget gives 2.92 solar masses for a 2.7 solar-mass gravitational configuration and a maximum of 3.33, so a remnant of 2.8 to 3.0 baryonic solar masses fits on the branch. This makes survival possible, not preferred, and both outcomes are stated with their tests. The core baryon parameterisation is a modelling choice, and the quoted budget is its conservative end.

Late-time observations then force a surviving remnant into a narrow class: a polar field below about 1.4e10 G at a one-millisecond period, or an ultra-early spin-down above 1e15 G depositing at most a few percent of a rotational reservoir of 4 to 5e52 erg. The large-scale coherence of that dipole is an open assumption with no supporting simulation.

The rotational estimates carry their own limits. The parameter T/|W| of 0.042 to 0.067 at a one-millisecond period is Newtonian with the relativistic factor varied by hand; the instability thresholds are standard uniform-rotation values; the r-mode energy uses a linear normalisation without nonlinear saturation; the field of 6e18 G described as virial is a scale estimate. The bar mode is absent throughout the range examined. The retrospective gravitational-wave amplitude sits at 0.065 of the relevant published limit, which means the observation did not test the model rather than that it supported it.

## Model assumptions

The corner-branch modes were computed on an analogue equation of state whose core is incompressible with a mass profile matching the de Sitter profile exactly. The pressure inside that core and its own eigenmodes differ from the intended vacuum-core problem, which has not been solved.

Perturbations were treated as test fields in the axial sector only, and stability was examined for radial modes only. Rotation follows a published stationary construction without a full perturbation tensor. Formation was modelled with prescribed accretion and a thin shell rather than a dynamical collapse. The moment of inertia used in the rotational estimates is a reference value, and the jet and neutrino luminosity normalisations are the standard quoted ones.

## Numerical limitations

Independent integrators agree where they were compared. Masses from a fixed-step Runge-Kutta integration and an adaptive solver agree to 1e-7 in relative terms and radii to 2e-4, with trigger masses agreeing to seven digits; the bounce curvature maximum varies by less than 0.01 percent across a factor of ten in step size, and the shadow impact parameter reproduces the Schwarzschild value to thirteen digits.

Two defects were handled rather than repaired. A numerical Kretschmann routine for the rotating metric returned negative values, impossible for that invariant, together with wild results near the axis; those values were withdrawn and the conclusion re-established with a validated implementation, but the faulty line was never located. Three attempts at a direct Gauss-Bonnet tensor computation were halted because the index structures were wrong, leaving that derivation incomplete. The stage-E2 amplitude table was kept as an unmodified snapshot and still carries the superseded echo values.

Several quantities are quoted to few digits because that is the precision the runs support, and part of the polar-sector output is preserved as captured console output rather than as a structured data file. A figure given here to two or three significant digits should not be read as carrying more.

## What would falsify or strengthen the conclusion

A detection of an echo train at the predicted delays, or a firm exclusion of echoes at reflectivities below about 0.6 across the branch mass range, would settle the horizonless question at the near-maximal end. A measured tidal deformability for a mass-gap object well above the branch prediction would exclude the branch at that mass. A collapse signature for the GW170817 remnant, or a maximum neutron-star mass established firmly below the branch threshold, would remove the most concrete candidate. A ringdown measurement reaching 0.1 percent fractional accuracy would test the halo criterion directly.

The conclusion would be strengthened by a derivation of the metric function from a four-dimensional action rather than from the inverse problem, by a polar-sector perturbation analysis with a genuine vacuum core, by a nonlinear stability analysis beyond radial modes, and by an independent determination of the transition density from nuclear physics rather than from the observed emptiness of the gap.
